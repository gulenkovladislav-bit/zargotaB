# Dialogue craft and playable causality

Applies to every Zargota episode, including AI branch creation, edits and manual
authoring. This is a review procedure, not permission to replace approved prose.

## Why the snake scene failed (2026-10-24)

The scene's `firstVisitDialogueId` named the nest before the character approached
it. A travel link also explicitly entered that node. Removing only the first
visit setting would leave a second route to the same premature observation.
The nest/wait dialogue belonged to an older mechanic: a visible snake departed
before collecting eggs. The current minigame conceals the snake until failure.
Both implementations were individually functional but told incompatible events.
Technical tests verified routing and rewards, not what Esteros could know.

## Before writing

Write a small private scene card: present location, observable evidence, prior
knowledge, immediate desire, obstacle, player-owned decision and possible outcomes.
Unknown facts stay unknown. Capture and inspect the playable scene before writing: arrival framing, the character location, the approach to the inspected object, and any close-up the player sees. Inspect the source image too, but never treat off-screen detail as already discovered. Check counts, movements, sounds and injuries against the actual sequence. Keep the screenshots with the task evidence.
Do not manufacture a movement, visible animal or elapsed wait to connect nodes.

## Review gates

1. **Arrival versus discovery.** Audit first-visit settings AND explicit travel
   entry nodes. A close inspection belongs to arrival at the object, not the map.
2. **A reason to think or speak.** What makes this particular person say this now?
   A need, an association, fear, resistance or an attempt to influence someone can
   carry information. A status report alone is usually better left to the UI.
3. **Player agency.** Do not announce a final decision before offering its opposite.
   Name choices plainly; do not promise a safe outcome or reveal hidden rewards.
4. **Voice and restraint.** Preserve vocabulary, rhythm and established emotional
   development. Melancholy is not an obligation to turn every object into a maxim.
   Ordinary practical thoughts and silence are allowed. Avoid decorative padding.
5. **Thought, action, speech.** A character's fear is not proof of a danger. A
   narrator's knowledge is not the character's knowledge. Keep attribution clear.
6. **Changed mechanics.** Re-read incoming and outgoing nodes after adding a
   minigame, item, reveal or animation. Remove obsolete explanations from the
   playable route rather than layering a second version on top.
7. **Read the whole branch.** Check cause and response, duplicate information,
   repetition on revisit, emotional portraits and both language versions. Review
   natural Ukrainian separately from literal translation of Russian.
8. **Verify in motion.** Enter normally and from the map, walk to the point,
   decline, return, succeed, fail and revisit. Automated checks establish timing
   and state invariants; they cannot certify the literary quality of a line.

Keep runtime changes narrow. Do not hide all first-visit dialogue globally to fix
one wrongly assigned observation. Keep item rewards and resolve events intact.
Old exports and initial-generation scripts are snapshots, not fresh editor state.

## Reading used

- Pauline Kiernan, [Writing Dialogue](https://www.theoxfordeditors.co.uk/about/free-advice-for-authors/writing-dialogue), read 2026-10-24. Practical takeaway: voice, rhythm and subtext should convey a character's inner life rather than explain it. Read lines as speech and check whether they belong to this person in this moment.
- Robert Yang, [notes on Jon Ingold's Sparkling Dialogue talk](https://www.blog.radiator.debacle.us/2018/11/notes-on-sparking-dialogue-great.html), read 2026-10-24. This is Yang's interpretation, not a transcript. Practical takeaway: a dialogue scene needs dramatic work beyond repeating directions. The linked original video was not accessible during this review.
- Jennifer Hepler, [The Exposition Burden](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1023352/The-Exposition). Only the indexed session description was available; the current page redirected to maintenance. Do not claim the lecture was watched.
